For completeness: (i) the confirmatory and sensitivity runs were executed as Docker containers running exactly the frozen-plan gate and the commands of \texttt{./run confirm} and \texttt{./run sensitivity}; (ii) infrastructure restarts interrupted the confirmatory run twice while HellaSwag was in progress; completed banks were kept unchanged, and the remaining banks and the sensitivity analyses were run with a checkpointing wrapper (\texttt{scripts/run\_benchmark\_resumable.py}) that calls the same frozen functions with the same seeds but saves each finished target, so that a restart only loses the targets in flight; on a test bank this wrapper reproduced the frozen runner's output exactly (maximum absolute difference 0); (iii) in the one-parameter sensitivity analysis on HellaSwag, one target produced expected response probabilities exceeding 1 by floating-point rounding, which made the frozen score-targeted (EVR) computation overflow and stop the run; the sensitivity runs from that bank on were executed with a guard in the wrapper that clips these probabilities to $[0,1]$ inside the EVR criterion, without modifying any frozen file; the guard is an exact no-op whenever the probabilities are valid, and it reproduced stored confirmatory and sensitivity outputs exactly (maximum absolute difference 0); the confirmatory runs had completed without it; (iv) unit tests for Propositions~\ref{prop:cap} and~\ref{prop:evr} and Corollary~\ref{cor:jensen}, the figure and table generator, the exploratory ranking analysis (Section~\ref{sec:ranking}) and the automatic check of the manuscript's qualitative claims (\texttt{scripts/check\_claims.py}) were written after the freeze; (v) the planned one-parameter sensitivity analysis turned out to be uninformative because its joint posterior mode is degenerate (Section~\ref{sec:sensitivity}); it is reported but not interpreted, and a variant with an informative prior on the common discrimination was added post hoc and is labelled as such; (vi) the late-test comparisons of Section~\ref{sec:curves} (plug-in levelling off, entropy crossover) and the mechanism analysis of Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism} were not in the frozen plan and are labelled exploratory; (vii) two post hoc experiments were added after all planned runs---strong baselines (Section~\ref{sec:strong}) and a regularization path of the discrimination prior (Section~\ref{sec:strong})---with decision rules recorded in the project's research log before running; they use the confirmatory targets, holdouts and panels, reproduce the recomputed confirmatory rows exactly, and do not enter the confirmatory decision rule; an exploratory decomposition of interval coverage into width and score error (Table~\ref{tab:covdecomp}) analyses the stored strong-baseline runs without new runs, with its interpretation rule also recorded before computing; (viii) the hash check of every frozen file was repeated after all runs (\texttt{PREREG\_GATE=PASS}); (ix) after an external review, every study-1 contrast was re-analysed with families as units (post hoc; Table~\ref{tab:clusters}), and a cost analysis and checks of the empirical-Bayes hyperprior were added (post hoc); (x) study~2 is a new study with its own frozen plan (\texttt{configs/study2\_plan.md}), not a modification of study~1; its interval-recalibration rule was chosen on development banks by a rule written before that development run (Section~\ref{sec:devhist}); (xi) because no external time stamp was available, the paper uses ``frozen analysis plan'' instead of ``preregistration'' for both studies; (xii) the prospective live protocol could not be executed (no access to model weights in the execution environment) and was replaced by a temporal split of SWE-bench Verified submissions, specified in the study-2 plan. (xiii) While study~2 was running, and before any of its results was inspected, an independent review of its code found two problems and three points of wording, and the handling below was recorded in the research log before the results were looked at; the frozen run was completed unchanged and every frozen decision is reported as computed. First, with eight calibrators the items have at most $2^8$ distinct response patterns, so tinyBenchmarks' K-Means can return fewer anchors than its budget, and the frozen code did not record how many it used; the number was recomputed post hoc with the same seeds (the recomputed errors reproduce the frozen ones exactly), and C8 is also reported on full-budget cells and matched on the number of items actually used (Section~\ref{sec:s2red}). Second, the burn-in of the fully Bayesian sampler (10 sweeps) was too short on some banks: the translation move is restricted to shifts that keep every sampled difficulty on the grid, so it rarely acts when many difficulties sit near the edge of the grid, and the overall location then drifted during the retained sweeps; both fully Bayesian arms were re-run post hoc with a burn-in of 60 sweeps at $N=8$ and $N=4$ (same targets, panels, holdouts and chains; with the frozen burn-in the re-run reproduces the frozen rows exactly), C3 is reported for both versions, and a joint fully Bayesian model was added as an exploratory arm. Third, the recalibration window is $[t/1.5,1.5t]$ (the plan says ``$\pm50\%$''), the translation move is exact only within its bounds, and the direction of a significant contrast is taken from the sign of the mean difference although the test is rank-based (as in study~1); \SignsDisc\ of the \SignsSig\ Holm-significant contrasts of the two studies have a mean difference and a rank-biserial correlation of opposite signs. (xiv) After a second external review, three robustness follow-ups were planned in the research log before they were run (Section~\ref{sec:s2follow}): panels drawn under cluster rules that do not use the target's responses and under other residual thresholds (RF1; the recomputed frozen clusters reproduce every frozen row exactly), longer multi-chain runs of the Bayesian CATs with convergence diagnostics (RF2; on a subset of 48 panels), and a random-effects meta-analysis across benchmarks (RF3); none changes a frozen decision, and the frozen arm called the fully Bayesian CAT in the plan is called the posterior-draw marginal Bayesian CAT in the paper.
